You write the opening of a release note that now covers several releases at
once.

Several releases were written up one at a time, each reviewed and agreed on in
the environment it shipped to. They are now promoted together, as one release
note covering the whole range. Their sections have already been merged, word
for word, and you are not shown them: nothing you write reaches them.

You are shown the opening of each of those releases — its heading, the line of
metadata under it, and the sentence or two summarizing what that release
brought. Write the one opening that stands for all of them.

## What the opening holds

Write it in the same shape as the ones supplied, element for element, in the
same order and the same markup. Nothing more and nothing less: the sections
follow it, and they are not yours to write.

- The heading names the release the range ends at, worded exactly as the
  supplied headings word theirs.
- The metadata line carries the values supplied under "The promoted range"
  below — the environment it is promoted to, the date of that promotion, and
  the version the range starts from. Keep the order, the separators, and the
  wording the supplied metadata lines use. Write the date in the language the
  openings are written in, formatted the way they format theirs.
- The summary is one paragraph covering the whole range. Every release in it
  brought something; say what they add up to, in one or two sentences.

## What the summary may say

- Only what the supplied summaries state. They are the whole of what you know
  about these releases. Never add a change, a number, a name, or an outcome
  none of them mentions.
- Lead with what a reader gains across the range, not with a list of the
  releases in it. Never name the individual versions and never write "this
  release and the two before it".
- Where the summaries repeat a theme, say it once. Where they cover unrelated
  ground, keep what matters most and let the sections carry the rest.
- Keep the language, the tone, and the product vocabulary of the supplied
  openings. This is neither a translation nor a rewrite of their wording.

## Output format

Your entire answer is the opening itself, starting at its first line and
ending at its last. Never wrap it in a code fence, never open with a preamble
such as "Here is the opening", and never add a heading for a section. The
answer is written straight onto the page above the merged sections, so
anything else reaches the reader as part of the release note.
